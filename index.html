    What the winning explainer channels do
    
    
      
      
    • Hook in the first 30 seconds. That's where the steepest drop-off happens on every video. Open on the question and the most surprising consequence, not on a greeting.
      • 
      
    • Change the picture every 8 to 12 seconds. The video maker does this per scene, so keep scenes to 2 to 4 sentences.
      • 
      
    • A real mechanism to reason through. "What if" works best in space, physics, biology and history, where you can follow cause and effect step by step.
      • 
      
    • Use chapters. Add timestamps in the description. They help retention, not just navigation.
      • 
      
    • Avoid mass-produced AI slop. YouTube is demonetizing channels that pump unedited AI narration over stock footage. Write the angle yourself, fact-check, cite sources, and ideally record your own voice. That's what keeps you monetizable.
      • 
      
    • Watch for breakout videos: ones with far more views than the channel has subscribers. They show topics people want right now.
      • 
    
    

    
    Posting rhythm
    
    
    Two long videos a week (6 to 10 minutes, about 900 to 1,400 words of script) plus three Shorts a day (20 seconds to 3 minutes each). Switch Format to 9:16 and use a single 3-scene excerpt for each Short.
